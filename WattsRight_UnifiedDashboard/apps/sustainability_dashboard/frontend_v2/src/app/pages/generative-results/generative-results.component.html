How This Works

Surrogate-Assisted Pruning

Instead of evaluating your model at every possible pruning threshold (which would be extremely expensive), this dashboard uses a Gaussian Process (GP) surrogate model to predict how pruning will affect performance. The surrogate was trained on 16 language models across 6 architecture families and learns cross-model patterns in how perplexity degrades as weights are removed.

The system only needs to evaluate your model at two anchor points (unpruned baseline and maximum pruning at threshold 10) to predict the full pruning curve. This saves roughly 98% of the evaluation cost compared to exhaustive benchmarking.

How Metrics Are Calculated

FLOPs are computed analytically from the model architecture (layers, hidden size, FFN dimensions) and adjusted proportionally based on the fraction of non-zero parameters remaining after pruning. This is a deterministic structural calculation, not a prediction.

Energy (kWh) is estimated as a proxy derived from FLOPs. The baseline energy consumption is measured during the real benchmark evaluation, and energy at other thresholds is scaled proportionally to the FLOPs reduction. This provides a useful approximation, but actual energy consumption depends on hardware utilization, memory access patterns, and other factors not captured by FLOPs alone.

CO₂ emissions are a further proxy, calculated by multiplying the estimated energy by a regional grid carbon intensity factor (gCO₂/kWh) for the deployment location you selected. These factors vary significantly (e.g. France ~50 gCO₂/kWh due to nuclear, vs. Australia ~700 gCO₂/kWh). The resulting figure gives a directional estimate of environmental impact, not a precise measurement.

Latency and tokens/sec are measured during the real benchmark step using wall-clock timing on your hardware.

Training Data & Model Coverage

The surrogate was trained on models ranging from 124M to 3B parameters across the following families:

  • GPT-2: 124M, 355M, 774M, 1.5B
  • OPT: 125M, 350M, 1.3B, 2.7B
  • Phi: 1.5B, 2.7B
  • LLaMA 3.2: 1B, 3B
  • Qwen 2.5: 1.5B, 3B
  • Bloom: 1.7B  |  Pythia: 1.4B

All evaluations used WikiText-2 as the benchmark dataset. Perplexity measured on a single benchmark may not reflect performance on other tasks or domains.

Key Findings & Limitations

Research findings show that the surrogate achieves good predictive accuracy for known architecture families, even at model sizes not seen during training. For example, predictions for GPT-2 XL (1.5B, an unseen size within a known family) showed strong agreement with actual evaluations.

For unknown architectures, predictions can be extremely inaccurate. Bloom 1.7B (an unseen architecture family) exhibited a perplexity degradation spike of a magnitude the surrogate could not predict the actual perplexity increase was far larger and sharper than anything observed in the training data. This demonstrates that different architectures can respond to pruning in fundamentally different ways that a surrogate trained on other families simply cannot anticipate.

This is a critical limitation: when working with model architectures not represented in the training set, treat the predicted curves as rough directional guidance only, and always validate with a real benchmark before making any decisions.

Other important limitations:

  • Only magnitude-based (unstructured) pruning is used. Structured pruning or other compression techniques may produce different trade-offs.
  • Models larger than ~3B parameters were not included in training. Predictions for significantly larger models are extrapolations and should be treated with caution.
  • Energy and CO₂ values are approximations based on proxies, not direct physical measurements at every threshold. They are useful for comparing relative impact across thresholds, not as precise absolute figures.

Uncertainty Communication

Shaded regions in charts represent one standard deviation of prediction uncertainty from the Gaussian Process. Wider bands indicate lower confidence. The benchmark validation step lets you verify predictions against actual measurements at your chosen threshold.

Human-in-the-Loop Design

All pruning decisions remain under your control. The system provides recommendations (such as the knee-point threshold) as advisory guidance, but never automatically applies pruning. You choose the threshold, review the predictions, and decide whether to benchmark.

Final optimization decisions remain under user control.

Privacy & Data Handling

  • Uploaded models are processed locally and are not retained after analysis.
  • No training occurs on your uploaded models or data.
  • Model weights are only used for the duration of the benchmarking session.
  • Analysis results can be exported and the session data is cleared.
